How to read this
What it shows. Standard breadth measures from daily closes: the share of stocks above their 20, 50 and 200-day averages; new 52-week highs and lows (the day's high or low against the prior year); the share of stocks 20% or more below their 52-week high; and each stock's contribution to its index (its weight times its return). The labels on each index summarize how many members are taking part. They describe the market; they do not forecast it.
History. S&P 500 lines use the members of the index on each date and start in 2018, when prices for past members are complete. "All stocks" uses today's list back to 2005. Companies that later failed are missing from it, so past stress is understated: the 2008 readings are a floor.
